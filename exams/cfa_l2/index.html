1. A ratio-based CAMELS analysis of a bank is least likely to capture:
  1. (A)the adequacy of the bank's capital relative to its risk-weighted assets
  2. (B)the bank's competitive position and the likelihood of government support
  3. (C)the proportion of the loan portfolio that has become non-performing
解析

(A)Incorrect. Capital measured against risk-weighted assets is precisely the C in CAMELS, and it is the component read most directly off reported regulatory data.

(B)Correct. CAMELS is assembled from reported, largely backward-looking data on one institution. Business model and competitive position, the mission and culture of the bank, and whether a government would stand behind it in stress fall outside the six components and have to be assessed separately.

(C)Incorrect. Non-performing loans as a share of total loans is a standard measure of A, asset quality, and is among the most closely watched CAMELS inputs of all.

CFA Level II | Practice item 100 | Financial Statement Analysis
2. An investor owns 25% of an associate and applies the equity method. During the year the associate sold inventory to the investor for USD 800,000; the inventory had cost the associate USD 600,000, and at year-end the investor still holds 40% of it unsold. The associate reports net income of USD 2,000,000. The investor's equity income for the year is closest to:
  1. (A)USD 450,000.
  2. (B)USD 480,000.
  3. (C)USD 500,000.
解析

(A)Incorrect. USD 450,000 eliminates 25% of the entire USD 200,000 profit. The 60% of the goods already resold to outside parties has been confirmed by a third-party transaction, so that profit is realised and stays in income.

(B)Correct. The sale is upstream, so the profit sits in the associate's reported income. Of the USD 200,000 gross profit, 40%, or USD 80,000, is unrealised because the goods remain in the investor's inventory, and the investor eliminates its 25% share, USD 20,000, from USD 500,000. Under the equity method the same proportionate elimination applies whether the sale is upstream or downstream.

(C)Incorrect. USD 500,000 is the unadjusted 25% share of reported net income. Ignoring unrealised profit would let an associate inflate the investor's earnings simply by shipping goods that have not left the group.

CFA Level II | Practice item 76 | Financial Statement Analysis
3. A parent whose presentation currency is the US dollar is determining the functional currency of a newly acquired foreign subsidiary under IFRS. Which fact most likely indicates that the subsidiary's functional currency is the US dollar rather than its local currency?
  1. (A)The subsidiary keeps its accounting records and files statutory accounts in the local currency
  2. (B)The subsidiary's selling prices are set by the parent and denominated in US dollars
  3. (C)The subsidiary's labour and materials are sourced locally and paid for in local currency
解析

(A)Incorrect. The currency in which books are kept or statutory accounts are filed is a bookkeeping and legal-filing matter. Functional currency is determined by the economic substance of the entity's operations, not by the ledger currency.

(B)Correct. The primary indicators are the currency that mainly influences sales prices and the currency of the costs of providing goods and services. Prices set by the parent and denominated in US dollars point to a US dollar functional currency, which means the subsidiary is remeasured using the temporal method.

(C)Incorrect. Locally sourced and locally settled costs are an indicator pointing the other way, toward the local currency being functional and therefore toward the current rate method with a cumulative translation adjustment.

CFA Level II | Practice item 88 | Financial Statement Analysis
4. An analyst estimates the intrinsic value of a share at $62 while the share trades at $50 in the market. The $12 difference between the analyst's estimate and the market price is best described as:
  1. (A)the error in the analyst's estimate, because in an efficient market price equals value.
  2. (B)the sum of the true mispricing and the error in the analyst's own value estimate.
  3. (C)the true mispricing, because the market price is observable and the estimate is unbiased.
解析

(A)Incorrect. This is the special case of a perfectly efficient market, in which price equals value and the whole gap is estimation error. Active analysts value shares precisely because they do not accept that assumption everywhere.

(B)Correct. Perceived mispricing, the estimated value less the price, decomposes into the true mispricing, true value less price, and the analyst's valuation error, estimated value less true value. Neither component is observable on its own, which is why a large apparent discount is never proof of a bargain.

(C)Incorrect. This would hold only if the analyst's estimate happened to equal true intrinsic value. Price being observable says nothing about the accuracy of the estimate placed beside it.

CFA Level II | Practice item 124 | Equity Valuation
5. A company's free cash flow to the firm for the year is $240 million. Interest expense is $40 million, the marginal tax rate is 30%, and during the year the company issued $70 million of new debt and repaid $45 million of maturing debt. Free cash flow to equity is closest to:
  1. (A)$237 million.
  2. (B)$225 million.
  3. (C)$293 million.
解析

(A)Correct. FCFE = FCFF - interest x (1 - tax rate) + net borrowing = $240 - $40 x 0.70 + ($70 - $45) = $237 million. Net borrowing is issuance less repayment, not gross issuance.

(B)Incorrect. $225 million deducts the full $40 million of interest. Because interest is tax-deductible, only $28 million of it is a genuine after-tax claim ranking ahead of the shareholders.

(C)Incorrect. $293 million adds the after-tax interest rather than subtracting it. Interest is paid out to debt holders and therefore reduces the cash left over for equity.

CFA Level II | Practice item 136 | Equity Valuation
6. A company is expected to earn a return on equity of 12.5% and to retain 40% of its earnings indefinitely. Its required return on equity is 11%. Based on the Gordon growth model, the justified leading price-to-earnings ratio is closest to:
  1. (A)10.5.
  2. (B)6.7.
  3. (C)10.0.
解析

(A)Incorrect. 10.5 is the justified trailing P/E, which multiplies the payout ratio by (1 + g) because it is expressed on last year's earnings: 0.60 x 1.05 / 0.06. The question asks for the leading multiple.

(B)Incorrect. 6.7 results from dividing the retention ratio rather than the payout ratio by (r - g). The numerator of a justified P/E is the fraction of earnings paid out, since that is what is discounted.

(C)Correct. Sustainable growth is ROE times the retention ratio, 12.5% x 0.40 = 5.0%. The justified leading P/E is the payout ratio divided by (r - g): 0.60 / (0.11 - 0.05) = 10.0.

CFA Level II | Practice item 148 | Equity Valuation
7. Two annually compounded government spot rates are observed: the two-year spot rate is 3.00% and the three-year spot rate is 6.00%. Under the forward pricing model, the one-year forward rate two years from today is closest to:
  1. (A)12.26%.
  2. (B)6.00%.
  3. (C)12.00%.
解析

(A)Correct. The forward rate model requires (1 + s3)^3 = (1 + s2)^2 x [1 + f(2,1)], so f(2,1) = 1.06^3 / 1.03^2 - 1 = 1.191016 / 1.060900 - 1 = 12.26%. Rolling a two-year zero at that forward must return the same as a three-year zero, or an arbitrage exists.

(B)Incorrect. 6.00% is the three-year spot rate, the average annualized return over all three years. The forward rate applies to the third year alone, and when the curve slopes upward it must sit above the spot rate that averages it in.

(C)Incorrect. 12.00% is the linear approximation 3 x 6.00% - 2 x 3.00%, which ignores compounding. It is a fair sanity check but here it understates the true forward by 26 bps.

CFA Level II | Practice item 160 | Fixed Income
8. A bond portfolio has key rate durations of 0.50 at the 2-year point, 1.50 at the 5-year point and 3.00 at the 10-year point. The 2-year rate falls by 50 bps, the 5-year rate is unchanged and the 10-year rate rises by 50 bps. The estimated change in the portfolio's value is closest to:
  1. (A)0.00%.
  2. (B)-2.50%.
  3. (C)-1.25%.
解析

(A)Incorrect. 0.00% assumes the two opposite 50 bp moves cancel, which holds only if the exposures at the 2-year and 10-year points are equal. The portfolio's 10-year exposure is six times its 2-year exposure, so the steepening costs it money.

(B)Incorrect. -2.50% applies the portfolio's total effective duration of 5.00 to a 50 bp rise across the whole curve. That is a pure level shift, and it overstates the loss here because the short end actually rallied.

(C)Correct. The estimate sums the key rate contributions: -[0.50 x (-0.50%) + 1.50 x 0 + 3.00 x (+0.50%)] = -(-0.25% + 1.50%) = -1.25%. Effective duration cannot produce this figure because the move is a steepening rather than a change in the level of the curve.

CFA Level II | Practice item 172 | Fixed Income
9. A rating agency's practice of assigning issuer ratings on a through-the-cycle basis most likely results in:
  1. (A)greater accuracy over short horizons but more frequent upgrades and downgrades of issuers.
  2. (B)ratings that are equivalent to a point-in-time cardinal estimate of the probability of default.
  3. (C)greater rating stability but a weaker ability to signal a change in near-term default risk.
解析

(A)Incorrect. Reacting quickly to current conditions, and so migrating often, describes a point-in-time approach such as a credit score. Through-the-cycle ratings trade exactly that responsiveness away in exchange for stability.

(B)Incorrect. A rating is an ordinal ranking of relative creditworthiness, not a cardinal probability of default, and by construction it is not a point-in-time measure.

(C)Correct. A through-the-cycle rating deliberately looks past temporary, cyclical swings in a borrower's condition, so it migrates less often. That stability is bought at the cost of timeliness: the rating lags a genuine deterioration in near-term default risk.

CFA Level II | Practice item 184 | Fixed Income
10. Maya Oduya, CFA, is licensed in Country A, works from an office in Country B, and manages portfolios for clients resident in Country C. Country B's securities law is less strict than the Code and Standards; Country C's law, which governs the accounts she manages, is stricter than both. Under Standard I(A) Knowledge of the Law, Oduya must most likely follow:
  1. (A)the Code and Standards, because they supersede any local securities law.
  2. (B)Country B's law, because members follow the law where they conduct business.
  3. (C)Country C's law, because it is the strictest of the applicable requirements.
解析

(A)Incorrect. The Code and Standards displace law that is less strict, but they do not displace a stricter applicable law. The rule selects the strictest requirement, not the Code by default.

(B)Incorrect. The location of the office does not settle the question. Applicable law is the law that governs the conduct in question, and here that is the law of the clients' jurisdiction.

(C)Correct. Standard I(A) requires compliance with the more strict of applicable law, rules, regulations, or the Code and Standards. Country C's law applies to these accounts and imposes the highest requirement, so it sets the floor.

CFA Level II | Practice item 1 | Ethical and Professional Standards
11. Tomas Berg, CFA, learns that a colleague routinely misdates client order tickets, which breaches local securities regulation. Berg reports this in writing to his supervisor and to compliance, but two months later the practice continues and Berg is still assigned to the affected accounts. Which action is most consistent with Standard I(A) Knowledge of the Law?
  1. (A)Report the colleague to the securities regulator, as the Code requires.
  2. (B)Dissociate from the activity, seeking a transfer or resigning if necessary.
  3. (C)Continue on the accounts while documenting his objections in a file.
解析

(A)Incorrect. The Code encourages but does not require reporting to regulatory authorities. Local law may compel it, but the Code's own obligation is to stop participating.

(B)Correct. Standard I(A) bars knowing participation or assistance in a violation. Once internal escalation has failed, the member must dissociate, which can extend to removing himself from the activity or leaving the employer.

(C)Incorrect. A documented objection is not dissociation. Remaining part of an activity he knows to be a violation is itself participation, which Standard I(A) prohibits.

CFA Level II | Practice item 2 | Ethical and Professional Standards
12. Anika Rao, CFA, runs an independent research boutique. A small-cap issuer pays her a flat fee, agreed before the work begins and not contingent on her conclusions, to write a report on the issuer. She publishes the report with a prominent statement that the issuer commissioned and paid for it. Rao has most likely:
  1. (A)violated Standard I(B), because payment from a covered issuer destroys objectivity.
  2. (B)violated Standard VI(A), because the fee must be disclosed to the issuer's shareholders.
  3. (C)not violated the Code, because the fee is flat and the arrangement is disclosed.
解析

(A)Incorrect. The Code does not ban issuer-paid research outright. What it bans is compensation structured so that the analyst's conclusion affects her pay, and arrangements the reader is not told about.

(B)Incorrect. Standard VI(A) Disclosure of Conflicts requires disclosure to clients and prospective clients, not to the issuer's shareholders, and Rao has already disclosed the arrangement in the report itself.

(C)Correct. Issuer-paid research is permitted where compensation is a flat fee set before the work starts, is not tied to the conclusion or the share price, and the arrangement is disclosed. Those conditions preserve independence under Standard I(B).

CFA Level II | Practice item 3 | Ethical and Professional Standards
13. An ETF is trading at a price above the value of the securities in its underlying basket. The feature of the ETF structure that is most likely to close that gap is:
  1. (A)the fund manager redeeming investors' shares directly at the day's NAV.
  2. (B)the exchange obliging market makers to quote within a band around NAV.
  3. (C)authorised participants delivering the basket and selling the new shares.
解析

(A)Incorrect. Direct redemption at net asset value is the open-end mutual fund mechanism. ETF investors sell on the exchange to other investors; only authorised participants deal with the fund itself, and they do so in large creation units, usually in kind.

(B)Incorrect. Exchanges can designate liquidity providers with quoting obligations, but those obligations are framed in terms of maximum spread and minimum size, not a band around net asset value. It is the arbitrage profit, not a quoting rule, that anchors price to value.

(C)Correct. When the ETF is rich to its basket, an authorised participant buys the underlying securities, delivers them to the fund for a creation unit, and sells those shares in the market. Buying the basket and selling ETF shares pushes the two values together, and the arbitrage keeps working because creation units are available on demand.

CFA Level II | Practice item 232 | Portfolio Management
14. A factor portfolio constructed from a multifactor model is best described as a portfolio with:
  1. (A)factor sensitivities matched to those of a designated benchmark index.
  2. (B)the largest expected return available at a given level of total variance.
  3. (C)unit sensitivity to one factor and zero sensitivity to every other factor.
解析

(A)Incorrect. Matching a benchmark's factor sensitivities describes a tracking portfolio, which is constructed to reproduce the benchmark's exposures as a whole rather than to isolate one factor.

(B)Incorrect. That describes a portfolio on the mean-variance efficient frontier. Efficiency is defined over total risk and expected return, whereas a factor portfolio is defined purely by its exposure profile.

(C)Correct. A factor portfolio isolates one source of risk: it is built to have a sensitivity of one to the target factor and zero to all the others. That makes it the pure instrument for adding or hedging a single exposure without disturbing the rest of the risk profile.

CFA Level II | Practice item 243 | Portfolio Management
15. A researcher tests hundreds of signal specifications on the same price history and reports the best one. Cross-validation is most likely used in order to:
  1. (A)raise the significance level a result must clear to count.
  2. (B)rescale the reported t-statistic for the number of trials.
  3. (C)judge the selected model on data not used in choosing it.
解析

(A)Incorrect. Raising the hurdle, for example by demanding a higher critical t-statistic once many tests have been run, is a separate remedy for data snooping. Cross-validation changes which data the model is judged on rather than the threshold it must clear.

(B)Incorrect. Adjusting a test statistic for the number of trials is what a multiple-testing correction does. Cross-validation produces an out-of-sample estimate of performance and does not rescale any statistic for the size of the search.

(C)Correct. Searching many specifications on one sample guarantees that the winner is partly fitted to noise, so its in-sample statistics are inflated. Cross-validation rotates which portion of the data is held out, so performance is finally measured on observations that played no part in the selection.

CFA Level II | Practice item 254 | Portfolio Management
16. An analyst estimates ROE = 3.10 + 0.85(Asset turnover) - 1.30(Financial leverage), with both slope coefficients statistically significant. The coefficient on financial leverage is best interpreted as:
  1. (A)the proportion of the variation in ROE that is explained by financial leverage.
  2. (B)the expected change in ROE for a one-unit increase in financial leverage, holding asset turnover constant.
  3. (C)the expected change in ROE for a one-unit increase in financial leverage, allowing asset turnover to move with it.
解析

(A)Incorrect. The proportion of variation explained is measured by R-squared for the model as a whole, or by a partial measure; a slope coefficient is a rate of change expressed in the units of the variables, not a proportion.

(B)Correct. A partial slope coefficient in a multiple regression measures the marginal effect of that independent variable on the dependent variable when every other independent variable is held fixed; the influence of asset turnover has already been removed from it.

(C)Incorrect. That describes the simple (one-variable) regression slope, which mixes in the indirect effect running through asset turnover. Multiple regression exists precisely to strip that indirect effect out.

CFA Level II | Practice item 34 | Quantitative Methods
17. A dealer quotes USD/EUR at 1.2000 - 1.2010 and USD/GBP at 1.5000 - 1.5018, where each quote is US dollars per unit of the base currency. A client wishes to buy British pounds and pay for them in euros. The EUR/GBP rate (euros per pound) the dealer will apply is closest to:
  1. (A)1.2515.
  2. (B)1.2490.
  3. (C)1.2500.
解析

(A)Correct. The client buys GBP from the dealer at the USD/GBP ask of 1.5018 and funds those dollars by selling euros to the dealer at the USD/EUR bid of 1.2000. Chaining the two ask sides, Ask(EUR/GBP) = Ask(EUR/USD) x Ask(USD/GBP) = (1 / 1.2000) x 1.5018 = 1.2515.

(B)Incorrect. 1.5000 / 1.2010 = 1.2490 is the dealer's bid on EUR/GBP, the rate at which the client could sell pounds for euros. Applying the bid to a client purchase would hand the client the spread, which no dealer does.

(C)Incorrect. 1.5000 / 1.2000 = 1.2500 matches bid with bid. A cross rate must chain the two legs in the direction the transaction actually runs; pairing the same side of both quotes eliminates the dealer's spread entirely.

CFA Level II | Practice item 52 | Economics
18. An analyst argues that investors prefer a dollar of dividends today to a dollar of expected capital gain, and that a company can therefore lower its cost of equity by raising its payout ratio. This argument is best described as:
  1. (A)Miller and Modigliani's dividend-irrelevance proposition.
  2. (B)the bird-in-the-hand argument for dividend relevance.
  3. (C)the tax-aversion argument for a low payout ratio.
解析

(A)Incorrect. Miller and Modigliani hold that with no taxes or transaction costs payout policy cannot change value at all, because an investor can manufacture a homemade dividend by selling shares, or undo one by reinvesting it.

(B)Correct. The Gordon-Lintner view holds that a dividend already in hand is less risky than an uncertain future capital gain, so investors apply a lower required return on equity to a high-payout company and value it more highly.

(C)Incorrect. Tax aversion argues the opposite: where dividend income is taxed more heavily than capital gains, investors prefer earnings to be retained or returned through buybacks, which favours a low payout ratio.

CFA Level II | Practice item 106 | Corporate Issuers
19. A stock trades at 50.00. The stock will pay a single dividend of 2.00 exactly one year from today, immediately before a one-year forward contract on it expires, and the one-year risk-free rate is 5.0% (annually compounded). The no-arbitrage forward price is closest to:
  1. (A)50.40.
  2. (B)50.50.
  3. (C)52.50.
解析

(A)Incorrect. 50.40 is (50.00 - 2.00)(1.05), which subtracts the dividend from the spot price and then compounds. That treats the dividend as if it were paid today; because it is paid at expiration, only its future value of 2.00 is deducted.

(B)Correct. The carry-arbitrage model gives F0(T) = S0(1 + r)^T minus the future value of the carry benefits: 50.00(1.05) - 2.00 = 52.50 - 2.00 = 50.50. A dividend is a benefit the holder of the asset receives but the long of the forward does not, so it lowers the forward price.

(C)Incorrect. 52.50 is S0(1 + r)^T, the forward price for an otherwise identical stock that pays no dividend. It charges the long for a cash flow he will never receive, and a trader could arbitrage it by buying the stock and selling the forward.

CFA Level II | Practice item 196 | Derivatives
20. An appraiser must value a recently completed, purpose-built research laboratory. The building is owner-occupied, no comparable laboratory has traded in the market, and no rental evidence for such space exists. The valuation approach the appraiser is most likely to rely on is best described as:
  1. (A)the cost approach, since replacement cost less depreciation plus land value can be estimated without sales or rental evidence.
  2. (B)the sales comparison approach, since adjusting the prices of recent transactions is the most reliable route to market value.
  3. (C)the income approach, since capitalising the property's net operating income directly reflects an investor's required return.
解析

(A)Correct. The cost approach values the site and adds the depreciated replacement cost of the improvements, so it needs neither comparable sales nor market rents. It is the standard fallback for new, unusual or special-purpose buildings, and it also sets a practical ceiling on value, since a buyer would not pay more than the cost of building anew.

(B)Incorrect. The sales comparison approach adjusts the prices of recently transacted comparable properties, so it fails exactly where the stem places the appraiser: no comparable laboratory has traded. It is the preferred approach only in an active market with frequent, similar transactions.

(C)Incorrect. The income approach capitalises rental income and so requires observable market rents. An owner-occupied, purpose-built laboratory produces none, and inventing a hypothetical rent for a building with no rental market would drive the entire valuation.
