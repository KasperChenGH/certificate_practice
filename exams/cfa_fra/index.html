1. Net income is USD 120, beginning total assets are USD 900, and ending total assets are USD 1,100. Return on assets computed on average assets is:
  1. (A)12.0%.
  2. (B)13.3%.
  3. (C)10.9%.
解析

(A)Correct. Average assets = (900 + 1,100) / 2 = 1,000; ROA = 120 / 1,000 = 12.0%.

(B)Incorrect. 13.3% uses beginning assets only.

(C)Incorrect. 10.9% uses ending assets only.

2. An increase in a company's days of inventory on hand, holding all else constant, will:
  1. (A)lengthen the cash conversion cycle and increase working capital requirements.
  2. (B)shorten the cash conversion cycle by deferring the outflow to suppliers.
  3. (C)have no effect on the cash conversion cycle unless receivables also change.
解析

(A)Correct. DOH enters the cash conversion cycle positively, so slower inventory movement ties up more cash for longer.

(B)Incorrect. Only a longer payables period shortens the cycle; days of inventory on hand enters it with a positive sign.

(C)Incorrect. Days of inventory on hand is a direct additive component of the cycle, independent of receivables.

3. Cash flow from operations computed under the direct method compared with the indirect method will be:
  1. (A)identical in total, but presented with different line items.
  2. (B)higher under the direct method.
  3. (C)lower under the direct method because non-cash charges are excluded.
解析

(A)Correct. The two methods differ only in presentation of the operating section; the total, and the investing and financing sections, are the same.

(B)Incorrect. Presentation choice cannot change the amount of cash generated.

(C)Incorrect. Non-cash charges are excluded from operating cash flow under both methods.

4. Securitising and selling trade receivables near the reporting date will most likely:
  1. (A)increase CFO in the current period.
  2. (B)decrease CFO in the current period.
  3. (C)have no effect on CFO.
解析

(A)Correct. Converting receivables to cash reduces the operating working capital build, boosting CFO — but the benefit is one-off unless the programme keeps growing, and reversing it later depresses CFO.

(B)Incorrect. The sale is a source, not a use, of operating cash.

(C)Incorrect. Receivables are an operating asset, so their reduction flows through CFO.

5. A construction company has a USD 10 million contract with total expected costs of USD 8 million and recognises revenue over time using an input (cost-to-cost) method. If USD 2 million of cost has been incurred to date, revenue recognised to date is:
  1. (A)USD 10.0 million.
  2. (B)USD 2.0 million.
  3. (C)USD 2.5 million.
解析

(A)Incorrect. The full contract price is recognised only when the performance obligation is fully satisfied.

(B)Incorrect. USD 2.0 million is the cost incurred, not the revenue; recognising cost as revenue would report zero profit.

(C)Correct. Progress = 2 / 8 = 25%; revenue = 25% x 10 million = USD 2.5 million, giving USD 0.5 million of profit to date.

CFA Level I | FRA | Income Statement
6. Which of the following would most likely cause reported revenue to be recognised on a gross rather than net basis?
  1. (A)The entity earns a fixed commission per transaction arranged for the supplier.
  2. (B)The supplier, not the entity, sets the price charged to the end customer.
  3. (C)The entity has inventory risk before the good is transferred to the customer.
解析

(A)Incorrect. A fixed commission per transaction is a classic agent indicator and points to net presentation.

(B)Incorrect. Lack of pricing discretion indicates the entity does not control the good and is acting as an agent.

(C)Correct. Bearing inventory risk is a key indicator that the entity controls the good before transfer and is therefore the principal, reporting gross revenue.

CFA Level I | FRA | Income Statement
7. An analyst computing interest coverage for a company that capitalises a significant amount of interest should:
  1. (A)exclude interest entirely and use EBITDA-to-debt instead.
  2. (B)use reported interest expense only, since capitalised interest is not an expense.
  3. (C)add capitalised interest to reported interest expense in the denominator.
解析

(A)Incorrect. Substituting a different ratio does not resolve the misstatement of coverage.

(B)Incorrect. Capitalised interest is still a cash obligation to lenders; excluding it flatters the coverage ratio.

(C)Correct. Total interest incurred, whether expensed or capitalised, is the economically relevant charge; analysts adjust both the denominator and, if appropriate, EBIT for the depreciation of capitalised interest.

8. A company that consistently reports gains on the disposal of its fixed assets is most likely:
  1. (A)depreciating those assets too rapidly relative to their economic consumption.
  2. (B)depreciating those assets more slowly than they are economically consumed.
  3. (C)applying the revaluation model to those assets.
解析

(A)Correct. A systematic pattern of disposal gains means carrying amounts are consistently below realisable value, which points to overly short useful lives or understated residual values.

(B)Incorrect. Too little depreciation would leave carrying amounts above realisable value and produce disposal losses, not gains.

(C)Incorrect. Revaluation would move carrying amounts toward fair value, eliminating systematic gains.

9. A company increases its allowance for doubtful accounts from 2% to 5% of receivables. In the current period, this will:
  1. (A)have no effect on the income statement.
  2. (B)increase bad debt expense and reduce net receivables.
  3. (C)decrease bad debt expense and increase net receivables.
解析

(A)Incorrect. The allowance is established through an income statement charge.

(B)Correct. Building the allowance requires a charge to bad debt expense, lowering income, and reduces the net carrying amount of receivables.

(C)Incorrect. Raising the allowance percentage cannot reduce the expense.

CFA Level I | FRA | Balance Sheet
10. Under IAS 16, if an asset previously written down through profit or loss is revalued upward, the increase is:
  1. (A)recognised in profit or loss to the extent it reverses the prior decrease, with any excess in OCI.
  2. (B)recognised entirely in profit or loss, regardless of how large the previously expensed decrease was.
  3. (C)recognised entirely in OCI and accumulated in the revaluation surplus within equity.
解析

(A)Correct. The symmetry rule ensures the income statement recovers what it previously bore, and only genuine new appreciation bypasses profit or loss.

(B)Incorrect. Only the amount reversing the earlier expense belongs in profit or loss; routing the excess there as well would overstate earnings.

(C)Incorrect. OCI treatment applies only to the portion exceeding the previously expensed decrease.

CFA Level I | FRA | Balance Sheet
11. The net pension liability reported on the balance sheet for a defined benefit plan is:
  1. (A)the projected benefit obligation less the fair value of plan assets.
  2. (B)the accumulated benefit obligation less contributions made during the year.
  3. (C)the total of contractual future benefit payments.
解析

(A)Correct. The funded status — the defined benefit obligation net of plan assets at fair value — is recognised directly on the balance sheet under both IFRS and US GAAP.

(B)Incorrect. Contributions affect plan assets but are not netted against the obligation in this way.

(C)Incorrect. Undiscounted future payments overstate the obligation; the obligation is a present value.

12. Beginning inventory is USD 200, purchases during the year are USD 900, and ending inventory is USD 250. Cost of goods sold is:
  1. (A)USD 850.
  2. (B)USD 950.
  3. (C)USD 1,350.
解析

(A)Correct. The inventory equation gives COGS = beginning + purchases - ending = 200 + 900 - 250 = USD 850.

(B)Incorrect. USD 950 adds the ending balance instead of deducting it.

(C)Incorrect. USD 1,350 sums all three figures.

CFA Level I | FRA | Inventories
13. Taxable income differs from accounting profit because:
  1. (A)accounting profit is measured on a cash basis while taxable income uses accruals.
  2. (B)tax authorities and accounting standard setters have different objectives and rules.
  3. (C)companies deliberately misreport one of the two figures to reduce the tax they pay.
解析

(A)Incorrect. The two bases are the other way round: accounting profit is measured on the accrual basis, and tax rules depart from it item by item.

(B)Correct. Tax rules pursue revenue collection and policy goals, while accounting standards pursue decision-useful reporting; the two legitimately diverge.

(C)Incorrect. The divergence is structural and expected under any tax system; deliberate misstatement would be evasion, not the ordinary reason the two figures differ.

14. On the quality spectrum, financial reports that are GAAP-compliant but reflect biased choices within the standards are best described as:
  1. (A)lower quality, because the reporting is compliant but not neutral.
  2. (B)fraudulent, because the bias is deliberate and known to management.
  3. (C)high quality, because they comply with the applicable standards.
解析

(A)Correct. Compliance is a floor, not a guarantee; biased estimates and aggressive-but-permissible choices sit between high quality and outright non-compliance.

(B)Incorrect. Deliberate bias within the standards is not fraud, which requires fictitious transactions or outright non-compliance further along the spectrum.

(C)Incorrect. Compliance is only the floor; high-quality reporting also requires decision-useful, unbiased content.

15. Which of the following is an underlying assumption of the IASB Conceptual Framework?
  1. (A)Conservatism.
  2. (B)Going concern.
  3. (C)Historical cost.
解析

(A)Incorrect. Deliberate understatement conflicts with neutrality and is not an underlying assumption.

(B)Correct. Financial statements are prepared on the assumption that the entity will continue in operation for the foreseeable future.

(C)Incorrect. Historical cost is one of several permitted measurement bases, not an assumption.

16. In assessing the credit quality of a corporate borrower, an analyst would place the greatest weight on:
  1. (A)growth in diluted earnings per share over the last five years.
  2. (B)leverage and cash-flow-based coverage of fixed obligations.
  3. (C)the price-to-book ratio measured against sector peers.
解析

(A)Incorrect. EPS growth is an equity-holder metric distorted by share count and says nothing about capacity to service debt.

(B)Correct. Credit analysis centres on scale, leverage, and whether recurring cash flow reliably covers interest and maturities across a cycle.

(C)Incorrect. Price-to-book, however it is benchmarked, is a market-based valuation multiple rather than a measure of repayment capacity.

17. An analyst forecasting a company operating in a high-inflation environment should pay particular attention to:
  1. (A)the company's fiscal year-end, which sets the comparative periods presented.
  2. (B)the number of shares outstanding, which scales the reported per-share figures.
  3. (C)whether the company can pass input cost increases through to selling prices.
解析

(A)Incorrect. The year-end affects which periods are compared, not the company's exposure to rising input costs.

(B)Incorrect. Share count scales per-share figures; it says nothing about the operating impact of inflation.

(C)Correct. Pass-through capacity determines whether inflation is margin-neutral or margin-destroying, and it is a function of competitive position and contract structure rather than of accounting.

18. The primary role of financial statement analysis is best described as:
  1. (A)preparing the financial statements and selecting the accounting policies applied under the reporting framework.
  2. (B)verifying that the financial statements are free from material misstatement and reporting an opinion on them.
  3. (C)using financial reports to evaluate a company's past and prospective performance in order to support economic decisions.
解析

(A)Incorrect. Preparing the statements and choosing the policies within the framework is management's responsibility; the analyst is a user of that output.

(B)Incorrect. Examining the statements for material misstatement and reporting an opinion is the external auditor's engagement, not the analyst's.

(C)Correct. Analysis takes the reports as given and uses them, together with other information, to evaluate performance and support investment, credit, and other economic decisions.

19. XBRL is best described as:
  1. (A)a machine-readable tagging language that makes reported line items directly consumable by analytical software.
  2. (B)an audit methodology for testing the design and operating effectiveness of internal controls.
  3. (C)a set of recognition and measurement standards issued by the IASB and adopted by listed reporters worldwide.

20. A company acquires a 30% voting interest in an associate and exercises significant influence. Under the equity method, the investor reports:
  1. (A)a single-line investment account increased by its share of the investee's earnings and reduced by dividends received.
  2. (B)the investment at fair value each period, with unrealised holding gains and losses recognised directly in profit or loss.
  3. (C)30% of each of the investee's individual assets and liabilities line by line, with revenue and expenses combined proportionately.
解析

(A)Correct. The equity method carries the investment at cost adjusted for the investor's share of post-acquisition profit or loss, less dividends received.

(B)Incorrect. Remeasuring to fair value through profit or loss applies where there is no significant influence, or under a specific fair value election.

(C)Incorrect. Combining a proportionate share of each asset, liability, revenue and expense is proportionate consolidation, which is not permitted for associates.
