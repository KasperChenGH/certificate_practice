1. A tax-exempt foundation with a long horizon must fund an annual spending draw of 3.5% of assets and preserve the real value of its corpus. The role that equities are *best* described as serving for this investor is:
  1. (A)providing a hedge against unexpected deflation through a fixed nominal claim on the issuer.
  2. (B)providing capital appreciation that grows the real value of the corpus alongside the draw.
  3. (C)providing a contractual income stream that matches the annual spending draw with certainty.
解析

(A)Incorrect. A fixed nominal claim that gains in deflation describes a nominal government bond. Equities are a residual claim whose earnings and dividends fall in a deflationary contraction, so they hedge inflation better than deflation.

(B)Correct. Equities are a residual claim on corporate earnings, which over long horizons tend to grow with nominal output, so the asset class is held principally for capital appreciation and real growth of the corpus rather than for contractual income.

(C)Incorrect. A contractual, certain payment stream is the characteristic of fixed income or an annuity; equity dividends are declared at the board's discretion and can be cut, so they cannot be relied on to match a spending draw.

2. A broad capitalisation-weighted equity index has come to be dominated by a handful of very large constituents. The consequence this *most likely* has for a fund that fully replicates the index is:
  1. (A)an unintended concentration risk, since shocks to a few names now drive most of the index return.
  2. (B)an unintended value tilt, since the largest constituents trade at systematically lower price multiples.
  3. (C)an unintended tracking error, since full replication cannot hold the largest constituents at index weight.
解析

(A)Correct. Capitalisation weighting sets weights by market value, not by any diversification objective, so when a few issuers grow very large the index and any replicating fund inherit an idiosyncratic concentration the investor never chose.

(B)Incorrect. Size and value are separate factors and there is no mechanical link between being large and being cheap; a concentrated large-cap index has historically been as likely to carry a growth tilt as a value one.

(C)Incorrect. Holding the largest, most liquid constituents at index weight is the easy part of replication. Tracking error in an index fund comes from fees, trading costs and cash, not from an inability to buy mega-caps.

3. A researcher tests 400 candidate signals on one historical sample and reports the three with the highest backtested information ratios. The principal flaw in this process is *best* described as:
  1. (A)survivorship bias, since firms that failed during the sample period were dropped from the database.
  2. (B)look-ahead bias, since data were used at a date before they would actually have been available.
  3. (C)data snooping, since the reported results reflect the number of tests run as much as any real signal.
解析

(A)Incorrect. Survivorship bias is a defect in the database, arising when failed or delisted companies are absent from the history. It is a real problem but is caused by data construction, not by the number of specifications tested.

(B)Incorrect. Look-ahead bias arises when a backtest uses information at a date before it was publicly known, such as ranking on annual figures that were only reported months later. It concerns timing of data, not multiple testing.

(C)Correct. Selecting the best of many tests on the same data makes the reported statistics conditional on the search: with 400 trials a few will look strong purely by chance, so the in-sample information ratio is upward biased and does not survive out of sample.

4. A manager sets country and sector weights from a macroeconomic and market-cycle forecast, then fills each bucket with broadly diversified baskets of names. This approach is *best* described as:
  1. (A)bottom-up, with return driven mainly by the manager's security-by-security fundamental research.
  2. (B)factor-based, with return driven by deliberate exposures to styles such as value and momentum.
  3. (C)top-down, with return driven mainly by the allocation decision rather than by security selection.
解析

(A)Incorrect. A bottom-up process starts from the individual company, and any sector or country tilt emerges as a residue of stock-level decisions rather than being set first.

(B)Incorrect. A factor-based process targets exposures to defined return drivers such as value, momentum or quality, which cut across sectors and countries; it is not organised around a macroeconomic forecast.

(C)Correct. A top-down process forms views at the aggregate level, on economies, sectors or the cycle, and implements them through allocation; because the buckets are filled with diversified baskets, almost all of the active risk sits in the allocation.

5. A manager is asked to track a broad bond index of several thousand issues while accepting up to 40 basis points of annual tracking error in pursuit of a modest excess return. The mandate is *best* described as:
  1. (A)active management, running a duration position materially different from that of the benchmark index.
  2. (B)enhanced indexing, matching the index's primary risk factors while taking small controlled deviations.
  3. (C)pure indexing, holding every issue at its index weight so that tracking error is driven toward zero.
解析

(A)Incorrect. Full active management is defined by a willingness to run duration and sector positions well away from the index, and it carries tracking error measured in whole percentage points rather than tens of basis points.

(B)Correct. Enhanced indexing keeps duration and the main risk factor exposures at benchmark but seeks a small edge from issue selection, sector tilts and trading; a tracking error budget of a few tens of basis points is characteristic of it.

(C)Incorrect. Pure indexing accepts no deliberate deviation and would not be given a tracking error budget to spend. It is also barely feasible in a bond index of thousands of often illiquid issues.

6. An analyst forecasting the coming year builds a model driven by the most recently published national accounts data. A colleague objects that the inputs are unreliable. The most likely limitation of official economic statistics for this purpose is that they:
  1. (A)are reported in nominal terms only, so they cannot be compared with a real return forecast.
  2. (B)are published with a lag and are then revised, sometimes substantially, after first release.
  3. (C)are compiled by private data vendors whose estimation methods are proprietary and undisclosed.
解析

(A)Incorrect. National accounts are published in both nominal and real (constant price) terms, and the deflators are supplied alongside them. The problem is timeliness and revision, not the absence of a real measure.

(B)Correct. Official series arrive weeks or months after the period they describe and are revised as fuller source data come in; index definitions and base years also change, so the number the analyst modelled may not be the number that ends up in the history.

(C)Incorrect. National accounts are produced by government statistical agencies, and their methodologies are published. Proprietary methodology is a concern with some private survey and index products, not with official statistics.

CFA Level III | Practice item 1 | Asset Allocation
7. An investor's economic balance sheet shows financial assets of 800,000 and human capital with a present value of 1,200,000. The human capital is stable and bond-like. The investor wants total economic wealth to carry a 30% exposure to equities. The equity weight required in the financial portfolio is closest to:
  1. (A)30%.
  2. (B)0%.
  3. (C)75%.
解析

(A)Incorrect. 30% applies the target to the financial portfolio alone, ignoring the 1,200,000 of bond-like human capital that is already on the balance sheet and already absorbing part of the investor's risk budget.

(B)Incorrect. 0% would follow if human capital were equity-like, since 1,200,000 of equity exposure would already exceed the 600,000 target. That is the right analysis for someone whose income tracks the equity market, not for a stable salary.

(C)Correct. Total economic wealth is 2,000,000, so the target equity holding is 600,000. All of it must sit in the financial portfolio, because the human capital is bond-like: 600,000 / 800,000 = 75%. Recognising human capital tilts the financial portfolio toward equities precisely because the rest of the balance sheet is bond-like.

CFA Level III | Practice item 15 | Asset Allocation
8. A university endowment with large unfunded commitments to private funds holds a dedicated reserve of short-dated government securities. The primary purpose of that reserve is to:
  1. (A)meet capital calls and spending without being forced to sell illiquid assets at a discount.
  2. (B)raise the portfolio's expected return by capturing the roll-down at the short end of the curve.
  3. (C)satisfy a regulatory floor on the share of assets an endowment must hold in government paper.
解析

(A)Correct. Capital calls arrive on the general partner's timetable, and they cluster in exactly the stressed markets where illiquid assets trade at their widest discounts. A reserve that can be sold at close to fair value converts a forced-seller problem into a planned drawdown.

(B)Incorrect. Short-dated government paper is the lowest-returning part of the portfolio, so the reserve costs return rather than adding it. That cost is the price paid for the option to avoid a distressed sale.

(C)Incorrect. Endowments are not generally subject to a minimum holding of government securities of this kind. Portfolio floors of that sort belong to regulated balance sheets such as those of insurers and banks.

CFA Level III | Practice item 21 | Asset Allocation
9. An asset class carries high transaction costs, and the investor holding it has a high tolerance for risk. Considered together, these two facts most likely argue for a rebalancing corridor that is:
  1. (A)wider on transaction costs but narrower on risk tolerance, so that the two effects largely offset.
  2. (B)wider, since trading is expensive and the investor is less troubled by drift away from target.
  3. (C)narrower, since trading is expensive and drift only raises the size of the eventual correcting trade.
解析

(A)Incorrect. High risk tolerance widens the corridor rather than narrowing it; it is high risk aversion that narrows it. Both factors here push in the same direction, so there is nothing to offset.

(B)Correct. Optimal corridor width rises with transaction costs and falls with risk aversion, so both facts point the same way. High costs make each rebalance expensive, and a risk-tolerant investor suffers little from the portfolio sitting away from target between trades.

(C)Incorrect. High transaction costs argue for trading less often, not more. Rebalancing sooner to keep each trade small simply pays the cost more frequently, which is the outcome a wide corridor exists to avoid.

CFA Level III | Practice item 27 | Asset Allocation
10. An investor holds a taxable account and a tax-deferred account of similar size, and the target allocation combines taxable bonds with equities held for long-term appreciation. Considering asset location alone, the investor should most likely place:
  1. (A)each asset class across both accounts in the same proportion as the overall target.
  2. (B)the bonds in the tax-deferred account and the equities in the taxable account.
  3. (C)the equities in the tax-deferred account and the bonds in the taxable account.
解析

(A)Incorrect. Mirroring the target in both accounts is administratively simple but forgoes the whole benefit. Location is decided after the overall allocation is set, and holding the same mix twice achieves that allocation with no tax gain.

(B)Correct. Asset location puts the least tax-efficient asset where the tax drag is removed. Bond coupons are taxed as income every year and cannot be deferred, whereas equity gains are taxed only on realisation and can be deferred for years, so equities lose least by sitting in the taxable account.

(C)Incorrect. This shelters the asset that least needs sheltering. Equity already enjoys deferral of its gain in a taxable account, so placing it in the tax-deferred account wastes that advantage while leaving bond income fully taxed each year.

CFA Level III | Practice item 33 | Asset Allocation
11. A fund holds 12,000,000 in cash that policy says should sit in equities, and the manager wants the market exposure immediately while the cash is being invested. Index futures are priced at 1,600 index points with a multiplier of 250 per point. The equitising position is most likely:
  1. (A)short 30 futures contracts.
  2. (B)long 7,500 futures contracts.
  3. (C)long 30 futures contracts.
解析

(A)Incorrect. A short futures position would leave the fund holding cash and a negative equity exposure, doubling the shortfall against the policy portfolio rather than closing it.

(B)Incorrect. 7,500 is 12,000,000 divided by the index level alone, with the 250 multiplier left out. Contract notional is the index level times the multiplier, which is 400,000 here.

(C)Correct. Each contract represents 1,600 x 250 = 400,000 of index exposure, so 12,000,000 / 400,000 = 30 contracts are bought. The cash keeps earning its own return while the futures supply the equity beta.

CFA Level III | Practice item 100 | Derivatives and Risk Management
12. Spot is 1.2500 units of domestic currency per unit of foreign currency and the one-year forward is 1.2250. A domestic-currency manager hedges foreign assets by selling the foreign currency forward, and the spot rate is unchanged a year later. The outcome is most likely:
  1. (A)a roll yield of about +2.0%, because a short forward position earns the forward premium.
  2. (B)a roll yield of about -2.0%, because the foreign currency trades at a forward discount.
  3. (C)a roll yield of about zero, because covered interest parity removes any gain or loss.
解析

(A)Incorrect. There is no premium to earn: the forward sits below spot, so the foreign currency is at a discount, and a seller of a currency at a discount gives up value rather than collecting it.

(B)Correct. The manager contracts to sell at 1.2250 and, with spot unchanged, gives up 0.0250 against a spot of 1.2500, or 2.0%. A forward discount on the foreign currency, which arises when its interest rate is the higher one, makes hedging costly.

(C)Incorrect. Covered interest parity is what creates the discount, by tying the forward to the interest rate differential; it does not neutralise it. Parity rules out arbitrage, not the realised cost of carrying a hedge.

CFA Level III | Practice item 106 | Derivatives and Risk Management
13. A holding trades at 100.00. A manager wants a zero-cost collar and finds the 92 put quoted at 3.10, the 106 call at 1.35, the 112 call at 0.55 and the 103 call at 3.10. The call to write, and the reason the cap sits so much closer to spot than the floor, are most likely:
  1. (A)the 103 call, because downside puts carry higher implied volatility than upside calls.
  2. (B)the 112 call, because put-call parity equalises premiums at equidistant strikes.
  3. (C)the 106 call, because a collar's floor and cap must be equidistant from the spot price.
解析

(A)Correct. Zero cost requires the call premium to match the 3.10 paid for the put, which only the 103 call does. Equity index implied volatility is skewed, so an out-of-the-money put costs far more than a call the same distance away, forcing the cap nearer to spot.

(B)Incorrect. Put-call parity links a put and a call at the same strike, not at different strikes, and it does not imply equal premiums. The 112 call at 0.55 falls well short of funding the 3.10 put.

(C)Incorrect. Nothing requires the strikes to be equidistant; they are set by whatever premiums the manager chooses to exchange. The 106 call raises only 1.35, leaving a 1.75 net debit rather than zero cost.

CFA Level III | Practice item 88 | Derivatives and Risk Management
14. A manager buys an at-the-money straddle on a non-dividend-paying underlying. Immediately after execution, the position's Greek profile is best described as:
  1. (A)delta near zero, gamma negative, vega positive and theta positive.
  2. (B)delta near one, gamma positive, vega negative and theta negative.
  3. (C)delta near zero, gamma positive, vega positive and theta negative.
解析

(A)Incorrect. Negative gamma with positive theta describes a short straddle, which collects premium and loses from large moves. A bought straddle carries the opposite sign on both.

(B)Incorrect. A delta near one describes a long forward or a deep in-the-money call, and negative vega describes a written option. The two long legs offset in delta while reinforcing each other in vega.

(C)Correct. The long call and long put have offsetting deltas near the money, but both are long convexity and long volatility, so gamma and vega are positive. Both legs also decay, which makes theta negative.

CFA Level III | Practice item 94 | Derivatives and Risk Management
15. A pension consultant is running a search for an emerging-markets equity manager. One candidate firm offers to fly the consultant's due-diligence team to its Singapore office on the firm's corporate aircraft and to host the team for two nights, arguing that a visit is the only way to meet the whole investment team. The response most consistent with Standard I(B) Independence and Objectivity is for the consultant to:
  1. (A)decline the visit altogether, because an on-site meeting with a firm competing for the mandate compromises the search.
  2. (B)accept the flight and the accommodation, provided the arrangement is disclosed to the search client in the final report.
  3. (C)make the trip but pay the team's own travel and lodging costs, keeping only the office visit itself.
解析

(A)Incorrect. On-site diligence is good practice, not a conflict; the standard governs who pays for it, not whether it happens. Refusing to visit would weaken the search rather than protect its objectivity.

(B)Incorrect. Disclosure does not cure the problem here. Accepting travel and hospitality from a firm still competing for the mandate creates the appearance of dependence the standard exists to prevent, and it does so before the recommendation is made.

(C)Correct. Standard I(B) requires members to protect their independence and to avoid situations that could reasonably be seen to compromise it. Paying the team's own way removes the benefit conferred by the candidate while preserving the on-site diligence the search needs.

CFA Level III | Practice item 109 | Ethical and Professional Standards
16. A private client's investment policy statement prohibits the use of leverage. Her manager identifies a levered closed-end fund that he believes would materially improve the portfolio's risk-adjusted return. Under Standard III(C) Suitability, the manager should most likely:
  1. (A)not buy it, and raise the constraint with the client so the policy statement can be formally amended.
  2. (B)buy it, because suitability is judged against the client's objectives rather than against her constraints.
  3. (C)take a small position, since a holding too small to change the portfolio's risk profile is not leverage.
解析

(A)Correct. Standard III(C) requires the manager to act in line with the written policy statement. A good idea is not made suitable by being good; the route is to discuss the constraint with the client and amend the document before acting.

(B)Incorrect. Suitability is judged against objectives and constraints together. Constraints are what translate a client's circumstances, liquidity needs and preferences into limits, and they bind the manager just as objectives do.

(C)Incorrect. A constraint binds whatever the size of the position, and the standard carries no materiality exemption. A small breach also establishes that the policy statement is advisory rather than binding.

CFA Level III | Practice item 115 | Ethical and Professional Standards
17. The head of a firm's trading desk is told by compliance that the desk's exception reports have not been produced for four months following a system change, and that nobody has reviewed the desk's trade allocations in that time. Under Standard IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors, the desk head should most likely:
  1. (A)wait for a specific allegation of misallocation before reviewing, so as not to imply distrust of the desk's own traders.
  2. (B)act at once to restore the review and examine the unreviewed period, since a supervisor must ensure the system works.
  3. (C)leave restoration of the reports to compliance, because designing surveillance systems is the compliance function's role.
解析

(A)Incorrect. Supervision is a preventive obligation rather than a response to complaints. Waiting for an allegation is the very failure the standard describes, and the absence of surveillance is the reason no allegation would surface.

(B)Correct. Standard IV(C) makes a supervisor responsible for ensuring that compliance procedures actually operate, not merely that they exist on paper. Once he knows the review has lapsed he must restore it and look back over the period that went unchecked.

(C)Incorrect. Compliance builds and maintains the systems, but delegating the task does not delegate the responsibility; the supervisor remains answerable for the desk. His knowledge of the gap is what triggers the duty to act.

CFA Level III | Practice item 121 | Ethical and Professional Standards
18. An institutional client instructs its manager to place a fixed share of the portfolio's trades with a broker that supplies the client with performance-attribution reporting. The manager finds that this broker's execution is consistently worse than the alternatives available. The manager should most likely:
  1. (A)tell the client that the direction may be costing it best execution, and record its decision to continue.
  2. (B)disregard the instruction wherever execution would suffer, since best execution outranks a client preference.
  3. (C)apply the research obtained to all client accounts, since soft-dollar benefits accrue to the firm's clients generally.
解析

(A)Correct. A client may direct its brokerage and the manager must follow the instruction, but the duty of loyalty requires telling the client that the direction may be reducing execution quality and documenting that the client chose to continue once informed.

(B)Incorrect. The commissions are the client's, and a client is entitled to direct its own brokerage even at a cost to execution quality. Quietly overriding the instruction substitutes the manager's judgement for a decision the client is allowed to make.

(C)Incorrect. Benefits bought with one client's directed commissions should serve that client; spreading them across the book has the directing client subsidising the others. Directed-brokerage benefits are not a firm resource to redistribute.

CFA Level III | Practice item 127 | Ethical and Professional Standards
19. An analyst produces three reports on the same equity portfolio: the first states the fund's time-weighted rate of return for the quarter, the second explains how much of the fund's 140 bp active return came from sector weighting versus stock picking, and the third explains how much of the fund's tracking error came from its exposure to the value factor. The second report is best described as:
  1. (A)return attribution, because it explains the sources of the realised active return.
  2. (B)risk attribution, because it decomposes the variability of the fund's active return.
  3. (C)return measurement, because it quantifies the return the fund actually earned.
解析

(A)Correct. Return attribution is an ex post exercise that links the realised return, or the active return, back to the investment decisions that produced it, typically allocation and selection.

(B)Incorrect. Risk attribution is the third report. It decomposes the variability of return — total risk for an absolute mandate, active risk for a benchmark-relative one — rather than the return itself.

(C)Incorrect. Return measurement is the first report — the calculation of the return itself, time-weighted or money-weighted. It establishes what the return was, but says nothing about why it arose.

CFA Level III | Practice item 70 | Performance Measurement
20. A benchmark-relative equity manager's risk model reports that the factor component of its active risk is 3.2% and the asset-specific component is 2.4%, and that the two components are uncorrelated. The manager's total active risk is closest to:
  1. (A)4.0%.
  2. (B)5.6%.
  3. (C)16.0%.
解析

(A)Correct. Uncorrelated components add in variance, not in standard deviation: 3.2² + 2.4² = 10.24 + 5.76 = 16.0, and the square root of 16.0 is 4.0%. A benchmark-relative mandate decomposes active risk this way; an absolute-return mandate decomposes total portfolio variance instead.

(B)Incorrect. 5.6% simply adds the two standard deviations together. That result would hold only if the factor and specific components were perfectly correlated, which the risk model has explicitly ruled out.

(C)Incorrect. 16.0 is the active variance expressed in squared percent, not a risk figure. It has to be converted back to a standard deviation before it can be compared with a tracking-error budget.
